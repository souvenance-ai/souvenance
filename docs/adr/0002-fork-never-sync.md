# Fork, never sync

A store is an individual: it is copied by forking (`memory fork <path>`, a consistent `VACUUM INTO` snapshot), never synchronized. We rejected sync — two or more stores converging through bidirectional writes — because the addition-only model makes a store a lived trajectory: supersede chains record truth from a lived position, and two diverged stores cannot merge without rewriting history. A fork is born from an exact copy of its parent — nothing added: if a fork should know its origin, its forker tells it in the child's own words — and then lives its own life; communication between stores stays read-only (exports, read-only mounts, citations in content). Restoring a dormant fork — an archive — is a rebirth, not a resync. `fork` deliberately does no scheduling and no transport: keeping copies off-disk and fresh is the installer's backup software's job; the verb is the copy primitive only.
